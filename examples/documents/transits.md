# Educational transit notes

A planetary transit occurs when a planet crosses in front of its host star along our line of sight. The measured stellar flux decreases during the crossing. Repeated dips can help estimate the orbital period.

Transit depth is the relative decrease in brightness. In a simplified model with a uniform stellar disc and a small opaque planet, depth approximately equals the squared ratio of planet radius to stellar radius. Limb darkening and contamination can change this relationship.

A light curve is a time series of measured stellar brightness. Instrument artifacts, stellar variability, and eclipsing binaries can resemble transit signals. A candidate signal requires additional validation before it is called a confirmed planet.
